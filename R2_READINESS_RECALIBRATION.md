# Coding-AGI Engineering-Readiness Recalibration

## Why this correction exists
The earlier internal rubric was unintentionally treated too linearly: a large new bounded capability could add many points even when the evidence came from a generated family designed by the same research process. That makes the scale inflate toward AGI without corresponding external transfer. The correction makes later points progressively harder to earn.

## Corrected working scale
These values are internal engineering heuristics, not probabilities of AGI and not scientific measurements.

- R2.49: **39.0/100** (historical anchor retained for continuity).
- R2.50: **40.5/100 provisional recalibration**.
- R2.51: **42.5/100 provisional recalibration**.
- R2.52: **44.5/100 corrected current baseline**.
- R2.53: **no automatic increase from mechanism-only synthetic evidence**. Any increase requires independent transfer that was not used to design the operator/reflex benchmark.

## New scoring discipline
1. Passing CI proves implementation consistency, not intelligence.
2. Passing a benchmark designed with the mechanism proves that mechanism under its declared boundary, not broad transfer.
3. Synthetic/bounded improvements normally earn zero to small provisional movement.
4. Large movements require unseen external task distributions, real repositories/environments, no generator access, and causal ablations showing the new mechanism is responsible.
5. Scores may decrease when stronger falsification reveals overclaiming. New versions are not guaranteed to score higher.
